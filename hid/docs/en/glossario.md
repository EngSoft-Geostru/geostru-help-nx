# Glossary

**Hydraulic invariance** — The principle whereby the peak flow discharged from an
area must not increase as a result of its transformation.

**Hydrological invariance** — Extension of the principle to the total volume, not
just to the peak flow.

**Runoff coefficient (φ)** — Fraction of the rainfall that becomes surface
runoff. It is about 0.9–1.0 for impervious surfaces, 0.2–0.3 for pervious
surfaces.

**Weighted runoff coefficient (φ<sub>pond</sub>)** — Mean of the φ values of the
individual surfaces, weighted by area.

**Weighted area (S<sub>pond</sub>)** — Equivalent impervious area, equal to
S<sub>tot</sub> × φ<sub>pond</sub>.

**Rainfall depth-duration-frequency (DDF) curve** — Relationship between rainfall
depth, duration and return period, in the form h = a·t<sup>n</sup>.

**Return period (T)** — Mean interval between two events exceeding a given
threshold. An event with T = 50 years has a 1/50 probability of occurring in any
year.

**GEV** — Generalised extreme value distribution, used to derive the curve
parameters as a function of the return period.

**Hyetograph** — Time distribution of rainfall intensity during the design event.

**Position coefficient (r)** — In the Chicago hyetograph, the relative position
of the peak within the duration.

**Rainfall losses** — Subtraction of losses (infiltration, interception, storage)
from the gross rainfall, to obtain the net rainfall.

**Curve number (CN)** — Parameter of the SCS method that summarises permeability,
land use and antecedent conditions.

**AMC** — Antecedent moisture condition of the soil: I dry, II average, III wet.

**Time of concentration (t<sub>c</sub>)** — Time taken by water falling at the
hydraulically most remote point to reach the outlet section.

**Hydrograph** — Time distribution of the flow at the outlet section.

**Nash model** — Representation of the catchment as a cascade of n linear
reservoirs with storage constant K.

**Attenuation** — Effect of a storage facility that temporarily accumulates water
and reduces its outflow peak.

**Discharge limit (Q<sub>u,lim</sub>)** — Maximum flow allowed into the receiving
water body, imposed by the regulation or by the operating authority.

**Head (H)** — Water depth above the discharge device, which determines its flow.

**Specific storage volume (w)** — Storable volume per unit area, expressed in
m³/ha, distinguished between pre- and post-development.

**Hydraulic criticality zone** — Classification of the territory (A, B, C) which
in Lombardia determines the minimum volume and the unit discharge limit.

**Development class** — Size-based classification of the development, which
determines the calculation method proposed by the regulation.

**Jurisdiction profile** — In HID, the set of rules, constraints and parameters
of a country or a region.
