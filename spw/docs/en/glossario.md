# Glossary

**Absolute elevation** — Elevation in the project reference system, in metres and positive upwards. It is not a depth.

**Active anchor** — Anchor to which preload is assigned.

**Active earth pressure** — Action of retained ground associated with sufficient wall movement away from the ground.

**Anchor bulb** — Length anchored in the ground that transfers force through bond.

**Assigned override** — Factor imposed by the user instead of the table value, with a mandatory reason.

**Backfilling** — Event restoring ground on the excavation side up to an elevation.

**Combination** — Set of rules and factors for a verification. In SPW NX it belongs to the project and follows the stages in its scope.

**Combination scope** — Set of stages to which a combination applies. The construction history remains connected.

**Concomitant action** — Value of another quantity in the same combination, stage and elevation as an extreme. For a section, moment, shear and axial force belong together.

**Diaphragm** — Continuous reinforced concrete wall constructed in the ground before excavation.

**Drained** — Ground behaviour in which pore pressures can dissipate according to the analysis assumptions.

**Elements** — Anchors, struts and loads defined once and referenced by stage events.

**Embedded retaining wall** — Wall embedded in the ground to support an excavation.

**Embedment** — Part of the wall below the excavation base.

**Envelope** — Collection of extremes across stages and combinations, with the condition from which each comes.

**Event** — A single change in a stage, such as excavation or anchor installation.

**FEM** — Finite element method. In SPW NX it represents the wall and ground through stiffness and yielding reactions.

**Final reinforcement** — Bars and ties summarised for construction after section checks.

**G1, G2 or Q category** — Action classification used to assign combination factors. G1 and G2 are permanent; Q is variable.

**GEO** — Geotechnical verification, such as passive resistance mobilisation.

**Governing condition** — Combination, stage and elevation from which an extreme or minimum margin comes.

**Ground surface** — Ground surface before excavation, defined on the retained and excavation sides.

**HYD** — Hydraulic verification associated with flow and piping.

**LEM** — Limit equilibrium method, used to study equilibrium and embedment with limiting earth pressure assumptions.

**Micropile** — Small-diameter element, often made of a tubular section and injected material.

**Mobilisation of passive resistance** — Measure of how much excavation-side ground resistance is engaged relative to that available in the model.

**Passive anchor** — Anchor without initial preload that reacts to displacements after installation.

**Passive earth pressure** — Ground resistance mobilised by a wall moving against the ground.

**Piping** — Internal ground erosion associated with water flow.

**Preload** — Initial force assigned to an active anchor.

**Retained side** — Side where ground is retained by the wall.

**SLE** — Serviceability limit state. For the wall it particularly concerns displacements.

**SLU** — Ultimate limit state, concerning structural or geotechnical safety.

**Spring reaction modulus (Ks)** — Parameter linking ground reaction to displacement in the FEM model.

**Stable identifier** — Element reference that does not change when stages are edited.

**Stage** — Moment in the construction sequence, described by ordered events.

**STR** — Structural verification of sections or elements.

**Strut** — Support working mainly in compression.

**Subgrade reaction** — Ground response to wall displacement in the spring model.

**Undrained** — Ground behaviour in which pore pressure dissipation is not assumed in the analysis considered.

**UPL** — Uplift verification due to water pressure.

**Uplift of the excavation base** — Loss of base equilibrium caused by water pressure.

**Utilisation** — Dimensionless ratio between demand and capacity. Values above one indicate exceedance in the context of the displayed check.

**Water table** — Water level in the ground. Its elevations on both sides affect pressures, effective weight and hydraulic checks.
